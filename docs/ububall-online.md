# Online Ububall implementation

Owner decisions: private invite matches; every signed-in player fields their own ubus. Deliberately closing or leaving the Discord Activity forfeits an active match immediately; unexpected network interruptions retain the 60-second disconnect grace before the connected opponent may claim forfeit. Disconnects pause planning and preserve the remaining Blitz time. Blitz expiry uses the latest server-saved draft, even if not locked. Opponent drafts remain private until resolution.

The online match shares the local game's meter and Blitz rules. Its server-side lifecycle handles move validation, private drafts, locking, deadline expiry, pause/resume and forfeit authorization.

Authoritative turn resolution validates both teams' moves and super eligibility, runs the existing 240 Hz physics for at most 12 seconds (1 second in Pulse, whose replays chain turn to turn from each turn's moving initial state), and returns the resulting state plus replay frames sampled every eight ticks. Scoring, end-of-turn meter and stun rules are shared with the local game. Tests cover deterministic resolution, input immutability, bounded replay, victory and super meter consumption.

Completed online play architecture:
- Dedicated `UbuballMatch` Durable Object per match, persisting state to SQLite and driven by alarms for turn resolution and timeouts.
- Authenticated HTTP and WebSocket routes enforce Discord session identity and exact Origin; player drafts remain strictly private until turn resolution.
- Trusted roster verification queries `MintRegistry` for owned ubus and validates equipped supers; guest teams generate server-side DNA.
- Symmetrical rightward-attacking client view transform flips seat 1 coordinates and angles seamlessly.
- Replay playback interpolates 30 Hz sampled frames, delays scoreboard updates until the goal moment, triggers fanfare, bursts, and super audio, and highlights scoring players.
- Strict control locking across moves locking, turn motion, opponent disconnects, and match completion; disconnects freeze Blitz planning and allow forfeit claims after 60 seconds.
- Heartbeats use the Durable Object WebSocket auto-response, so `{"type":"ping"}` never wakes a hibernating match; liveness uses the auto-response timestamp.
- Guests may host and join without Discord. Guest seats always field three server-generated ubus from the [starter deciles](mint.md#starter-packs), which they cannot keep, and cannot reach the mint; a guest who signs in with Discord in the same browser keeps their seat.
- Unexpected network interruptions reconnect to the current match. Standalone page reloads restore matches via `?match=<id>`; deliberate Activity closure ends its connection and match participation.
- Comprehensive test coverage in `test/ububall-match.test.js` and `test/worker.integration.mjs`.

## Queues and live spectating

Launching in Discord signs the player in to the lobby for that Activity, initially **Not ready**. The panel beside the rink shows four player cards per row: Discord avatar (initials when unavailable), name and **Ready**, **Not ready**, **Balling** or **Spectating**. Discord participant updates remove departed players immediately from the roster. Server-verified membership reconciles departures, and a departure request clears readiness, challenges, assignments and match/spectator connections. Each verified launch and browser connection has an identity: a new launch replaces stale membership, while old heartbeats, departures and delayed initial joins cannot revive it or affect the replacement. Presence expiry after 20 seconds is a fallback for lost connections, not the deliberate-leave mechanism. **Ready to play** immediately matches another ready player in that lobby, each with their last team, otherwise their favorites and newest ubus; **Cancel ready** stops waiting. Starting CPU play preserves existing readiness or a pending challenge. **Find Online Match** and choosing a match to watch cancel lobby readiness and pending challenges. Loading uses a spinner; routine connection/instruction prose is omitted and status text is reserved for errors. Public matchmaking keeps its separate queue for Activity and standalone players, and opens team selection once paired. Taking a seat by any route opens an unopened signup pack in the background, so every signed-in player has a team. The Activity shares the site's Play, Ubu Mint and Collection tabs: a match starting elsewhere returns to Play, and an incoming challenge marks the Play tab. **Change team** there only chooses the team, without starting a CPU match.

The spinner has a reserved slot immediately after **Lobby**; errors overlay the panel without moving its content. Phone layouts put the lobby below the rink, including landscape. Opening the pause menu moves the same live lobby beside the menu when both panels fit, or below it on narrower screens; closing the menu restores it without creating another lobby connection.

**Ready** and **Challenge** can run alongside CPU play without confirmation. The player can resume the CPU game while waiting. When a match is assigned, **Join match** ends the CPU game and enters the online match; **Keep playing CPU** releases that assignment and preserves the CPU game. Watching asks before ending a CPU match. Switching away from a live online match asks for confirmation that leaving forfeits it. Spectators can stop watching or switch activities without a forfeit. Pause-menu headings and actions distinguish CPU play, spectating, friend matches, public online matches and waiting queues.

Players may challenge Ready or Not ready members of their own lobby. A challenge can be accepted, declined or canceled and expires after 30 seconds or when a participant leaves or becomes busy. Acceptance reserves both seats together, preventing a competing queue or challenge from assigning either player again. Accepted challenges open the shared rules popup followed by team selection. Either player may change the rules during setup; changing them clears both players' readiness. Owned-team selection still requires server verification of the collection and supers.

Instant lobby matches show a prominent **Change rules** button until the first player locks their launch. Applying different rules creates a fresh game with those rules and the same players and practice teams, keeping their connections. The server serializes rule changes against launches, rejects outdated rules editors and rejects moves or teams from a previous game generation. Direct challenges retain the rules/team setup flow rather than starting automatically.

Opponents share a single roster card spanning two columns, including the intervening grid gap, with VS between the avatars. A pair never splits across rows. Clicking a player in an active match starts watching directly. Same-Activity spectating defaults on; either player can disable it from the pause menu, immediately ending spectator connections. External watch links require an explicit share action and are revoked when spectating is disabled. The viewer receives the public rink, scores and resolved turns. Draft moves, team selection and match controls stay with the players. Either player can revoke the link and end existing viewer connections.

## Discord test setup

1. Use a private Discord Developer Portal application and an isolated HTTPS origin that reaches the test Worker. Set `ORIGIN` to that exact origin and map the Activity's root URL to it. Enable Activities and the target platforms, then add the test accounts.
2. Set `DISCORD_CLIENT_ID` and `DISCORD_CLIENT_SECRET` for that application. Set its bot token as `DISCORD_ACTIVITY_BOT_TOKEN` in ignored local configuration or the Worker secrets. The backend uses the token for Activity membership checks. The existing standalone OAuth flow still uses its configured `/auth/callback`.
3. Launch through Discord and test with two players and a viewer. Confirm the embedded sign-in cookie and proxied WebSocket upgrade, test a mid-match join and revocation, and try backgrounding and resuming on mobile. Then queue one player inside Discord and one in standalone ububall.

Activity launch through Discord's live proxy and deployment require separate verification. See [Discord's setup guide](https://docs.discord.com/developers/activities/building-an-activity) and [networking guide](https://docs.discord.com/developers/activities/development-guides/networking).

Local verification (2026-11-05): `npm test` and `npm run test:worker` pass. Unit and isolated Worker tests cover challenge authorization/expiry, queue reservations, rule changes and stale moves, departure before join, immediate replacement, stale-request rejection, participant-snapshot races and viewer access/revocation. Chromium and Firefox fixtures verify desktop/phone card spans, challenge setup, instant rules changes, direct watching, deliberate-exit forfeits, participant removal and no further ordinary requests after disposal, with no browser script errors. Follow-up pause-lobby checks cover fixed loading/error geometry, phone ordering, the shared responsive pause lobby, CPU offer acceptance/decline, background CPU challenges and switching confirmations. Live Discord SDK/proxy lifecycle behavior and owner acceptance remain unverified.
